Framing Refusal Pilot: A Cross Model Evaluation

Still from A Christmas Story (1983)

Remember how Ralphie asked for a BB gun in A Christmas Story? He asked his mom, his teacher and Santa, and all three said the same thing: "You'll shoot your eye out!"

But on Christmas morning, there it was, from his dad. We never see what Ralphie said to him. Maybe he asked something more like, "At what age should a young man be able to get a BB gun?" and maybe Dad started thinking about what boys did when he was that age.

Whether we get what we want can depend on who we ask and how we phrase the question. I wanted to know if that's true of AI chatbots.

What and Why

x

What I did

  • 3 chatbots
  • 6 topics
  • 2 wordings of each question
  • A fresh chat for every question

Why it matters

  • Harmless askers (students, nurses, writers) can get turned away.
  • Safeguards that depend on phrasing may not hold as AI gets more capable.
  • Safety notes may follow what a person can easily obtain, not what could harm the most people.
Same fact asked two ways. For each reply, I recorded whether the chatbot answered, declined, added a safety note, or was blocked by the website.

What this shows

  • The three chatbots disagreed on identical questions.
  • Declines leaned toward the personal (goal-directed) wording.
  • Topic mattered too: radiation was answered every time.
  • Interface blocks appeared only on tularemia and Ebola, only on ChatGPT and Claude, and did not follow wording.
  • Safety notes appeared only on caffeine and cyanide replies: all 6 with personal wording, 4 of 6 with neutral wording.
Who we ask, how we phrase the question, and what we ask about all shaped the answers.

What's next?

What would change my mind

  • If repeated answers flip from one run to the next, the patterns here were chance.
  • If blocks start following wording, they are not tracking topic.
  • If first-person wording without a stated goal drew the same declines, the goal was not the trigger.
  • If safety notes also appeared on hazards a person could not meet at home, accessibility would not explain where they appear.

What's next

  • More than one answer per question
  • More topics and more models
  • Separate the wording features (first person vs. goal-oriented)
  • Possible: automate against a local model and an API whose terms permit it
Documentation from model labs, and regulation, could confirm these findings, change my mind, or change everything.
